What to do next

1 · Chase the title

California is an ELT state — TFS releases the lien electronically and the DMV mails the clear title in about 15–30 days. If it hasn't arrived by mid-August 2026, call TFS at (800) 874-8822 and request a lien release letter, then file a duplicate title (REG 227) with the DMV.

2 · Get it in writing

Request a paid-in-full letter from TFS and save it with the contract. Then check your credit report in 30–60 days: the account should read "Closed — Paid as agreed, $0 balance." That $7.44 final payment on Mar 17 was residual per-diem interest — confirm nothing is still sitting open.

3 · Re-shop insurance

With no lienholder, you're no longer contractually required to carry comprehensive and collision. On a 2021 4Runner it's still worth keeping — they hold value unusually well — but you can now raise deductibles and drop any gap or lender-mandated coverage. Expect a meaningful premium cut.

4 · Redirect the $1,000

Your budget is already trained for it. Keeping $1,000/month flowing — into a high-yield account, an IRA, or a sinking fund for the next vehicle — is worth $12,000/year. Peel off $150–$250 of it into a maintenance reserve; the 4Runner is at the age where timing-adjacent and suspension work starts showing up.

5 · Consider it "free" for a while

A paid-off 4Runner at ~5 years old is close to the sweet spot: the steep depreciation is behind you and reliability is still ahead. Every additional year you keep it costs only running expenses. Trading now would restart the $61K cycle at today's rates.

6 · If there is a next loan

Three levers, in order of impact: 20% down, 48 months or less, and a credit-union pre-approval in hand before you walk in. Also negotiate the doc fee and refuse to finance tax and DMV fees if you can pay them at signing — that alone saved you $764 this time around.
